# The Ancient Hebrew alphabet (Benner's Lexicon, pp. 5 to 6)

Transcribed from the user's printed copy. Hebrew is read right to left. Each letter carries a picture, a concept and a sound. Benner's reads a root by combining the pictures of its letters.

Notes from the book's text:

- The pictographic script is more than 4,000 years old.
- Aleph (ox head) stands for "strength", from the strength of the ox.
- Lamed (shepherd staff) stands for "authority", from the shepherd's authority over the flock.
- Aleph + Lamed read right to left give EL (אל), "strong authority".
- Ghayin has no modern Hebrew letter in Benner's table. It is the original second sound of Ayin.
- Vav, Hey, Yud and Aleph are the four letters that make child roots (see `benner-entry-guide.md`).

## How to use this table

Strong's gives the standard sense. To approach Benner's ancient reading, take the word's parent root (two letters, shown in Benner's entry), read each letter's picture and meaning from the table, and compare the result with Benner's Act, Obj and Abs ideas. Use the letters in the Strong's lemma only as a guide to the consonants. The parent root is the one printed in Benner's entry, not a guess from the table.
