# Reading a Benner's entry

From the "Identifying Words and Roots" page (p. 4) of Benner's Lexicon of Biblical Hebrew, in summary.

## Root levels

- **Parent root (pr)**: a two-letter root. All other roots and words derive from these. Shown as Hebrew, then an English transliteration, then three kinds of idea tied to the root:
  - **Act**: action ideas.
  - **Obj**: object ideas.
  - **Abs**: abstract ideas.
  - Then **Def**, the definition of the root, then connections to **Ancient Hebrew (AH)**, the pictographic letters.
- **Child root (ch)**: a parent root with one of the four letters aleph, hey, vav or yud placed in front, in the middle or behind it. In ancient Hebrew these four letters worked as both consonants and vowels.
- **Adopted root (ad)**: a parent root with any other letter placed inside it. Where known, **Rel** gives the relationship to the parent root.

## Words under a root

Verbs (**vrb**) and nouns derive from parent, child and adopted roots. Each has an identifying number in the left margin. This is the number the Strong's to Benner's table gives. It is an entry number, not a page number.

- Nouns are masculine (**masc**), feminine (**fem**) or, rarely, common (**com**).
- After the transliteration come a one or two word translation (**Tran**) and sometimes alternates (**Alt**), then the word's definition (**Def**), its KJV translations, and the Strong's number(s) it corresponds to.
- An Aramaic counterpart follows the Strong's number when there is one. If its spelling or meaning differs from the Hebrew, the difference is given.

## Layout of the book

- Main section: alphabetical listing by parent root.
- Then a section of adopted roots whose parent root is unknown.
- Then words derived from the rare four-letter roots.
- Back of the book: index of Strong's numbers cross-referenced to Benner's numbers (pp. 526 to 558 in the user's copy). That index is what `strongs-to-benner.csv` holds.

## How to use it with Strong's

Read Strong's first for the standard sense and KJV usage. Then open the Benner's entry by number and read the parent root's Act, Obj and Abs ideas and the AH connection. The gap between the two readings is the useful part for study.
